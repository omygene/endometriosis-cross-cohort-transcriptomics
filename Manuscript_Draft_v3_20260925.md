## Title (proposed)

**Benign immunoediting in endometriosis: an immune-silent, proliferation-arrested survival program defined by locked-threshold meta-analysis, single-cell localization, and external atlas validation**

---

## Abstract

**Background.** Endometriosis is a chronic, estrogen-dependent disorder whose ectopic lesions survive for years in a peritoneal environment rich in immune surveillance. In cancer, long-term tumor persistence under immune pressure is explained by immunoediting — elimination, equilibrium, escape — where the equilibrium phase enforces a dormant, non-proliferating, immunologically silent state. Whether a benign lesion can occupy an analogous state is unknown. **Ectopic endometriosis, we argue, survives by withdrawal, not by warfare.**

**Methods.** We performed a pre-registered, locked-threshold random-effects meta-analysis (REML) of six independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts of ectopic endometriosis versus eutopic endometrium (21,355 genes; BH-FDR < 0.05 as the sole claim gate), five pre-locked functional axes (cytotoxicity, antigen presentation, senescence/dormancy, proliferation, stromal ECM), single-cell pseudobulk localization in three scRNA-seq cohorts, external validation in the independent GSE213216 single-cell atlas (373,851 cells; 21 patients; patient-level pseudobulk), and pre-registered perturbational reverse-confirmation in the LINCS L1000 compendium (SERPINE1-knockdown and estradiol arms, with a GEO census of established endometriosis drugs and an exploratory dienogest series). Robustness was defined a priori as retention of Tier-1 membership under every leave-one-cohort-out and cohort-exclusion scenario.

**Results.** Adaptive immune-effector axes (cytotoxicity, antigen presentation) were consistently null across cohorts — an immune-silent state. A 485-gene Tier-1 core (121 up / 364 down) was shared across cohorts (median I² = 86.3% across all genes, reported unfiltered); within it, a **66-gene robust core** retained Tier-1 membership under every sensitivity scenario, composed of an immediate-early stress-response module (FOS, FOSB, EGR1) and an innate-inflammatory module (IL6, SOCS3, C1QB). The proliferation axis was significantly shut down (M = −0.806, FDR = 0.039) and concordant in the paired cohort. SERPINE1 was the only axis gene reaching Tier-1 (M = +0.733, FDR = 0.017, 6/6 cohorts concordant), localized to endothelial/perivascular subclusters in scRNA-seq. In external validation, the Tier-1 directional signature was positive in all 8 tested lineages and passed FDR in the mural (smooth-muscle/perivascular) compartment (Δ = +0.61, FDR = 0.0297; independent-patient sensitivity p = 0.0032), while the lineage-level SERPINE1 niche prediction was not confirmed and is reported as such. Pre-registered pharmacologic reverse-confirmation was negative at the locked gate in LINCS L1000 space (best FDR = 0.129), and the exploratory dienogest series showed no reversal — program reversibility remains unconfirmed and is deferred to endometrial cellular models.

**Conclusions.** Ectopic endometriotic tissue adopts an immune-silent, proliferation-arrested survival program whose robust core replicates across platforms, cohorts, sensitivity scenarios, and an independent single-cell atlas. This is, to our knowledge, the first demonstration of an equilibrium-phase-like dormant state in a benign lesion — benign immunoediting — and it reframes endometriosis persistence as an active, transferable tissue program rather than failed immune clearance alone.

**Keywords:** endometriosis; immunoediting; meta-analysis; immune evasion; cellular dormancy; SERPINE1; single-cell; external validation

## 1. Introduction

Endometriosis affects roughly 10% of women of reproductive age. Ectopic lesions implant, vascularize, and persist for years despite continuous exposure to peritoneal immune surveillance — a survival feat that classical inflammation-centered models do not explain. Two competing expectations dominate the literature: lesions are either chronically inflamed (immune-activated) or immunologically evasive (immune-silent). Transcriptomic studies have been small, heterogeneous, and often contradictory, leaving the question open.

Cancer biology solved an analogous puzzle two decades ago. The cancer-immunoediting framework (elimination–equilibrium–escape) describes how a transformed cell population persists under immune pressure: clones that cannot be eliminated enter **equilibrium**, a state of immune-mediated dormancy in which proliferation is restrained and immunogenicity is edited away, before eventual escape [Dunn/Schreiber refs]. The framework was built for malignancy and has never been formally tested in a benign lesion. Endometriosis is the natural test case: a benign tissue that must survive for years in an immunologically hostile environment without the option of metastatic escape. If an equilibrium-like state exists outside cancer, ectopic endometriotic tissue should display its three molecular predictions simultaneously: silencing of adaptive immune effectors, proliferative arrest, and a stable, transferable survival program. We term this hypothesis **benign immunoediting**.

A second methodological gap compounds the first. Prior endometriosis transcriptomics implicitly assumed a single homogeneous disease state: one cohort, one contrast, one list. Under that assumption, between-cohort discordance can only be read as failure — and it has been, repeatedly. We take the opposite position: if lesions sit at different points along an immunoediting spectrum, then between-cohort heterogeneity is not noise around a true mean but the measurable signature of the spectrum itself. We therefore report heterogeneity in full (median I² = 86.3% genome-wide) and require that any claim survive it, rather than explaining it away.

We reasoned that a disciplined meta-analytic approach — locked thresholds declared before any look at the data, heterogeneity reported rather than filtered away, patient-level single-cell validation, and a pre-registered external replication in an independent atlas — could settle which state ectopic tissue actually adopts, and whether that state carries a transferable molecular signature.

## 2. Results

### 2.1 Study design and locked analysis pipeline

Six bulk microarray/RNA-seq cohorts comparing ectopic lesions to eutopic endometrium met the pre-locked inclusion rule (minimum k = 4 cohorts per claim); the paired-cohort arm (GSE11691) was reserved for concordance testing and never pooled (Fig. 1a). The pipeline, thresholds (BH-FDR < 0.05 sole gate; |M| ≥ 0.5 interpretive only), and the five functional axes (24 genes) were locked before analysis (Fig. 1b). Heterogeneity statistics (τ², I², Q) are reported for every gene and never used as exclusion criteria.

### 2.2 Ectopic lesions are immune-silent: the adaptive-effector axes are consistently null

Across all cohorts, neither the cytotoxicity axis (CD8A, CD8B, GZMB, PRF1, NKG7, GNLY) nor the antigen-presentation axis (HLA-A/B/C, B2M, TAP1, TAP2) showed a consistent directional shift: pathway-level meta-estimates were statistically null, and single-gene effects were scattered around zero (Fig. 4a, Fig. 5a). Given that the same pipeline had ample power to detect the stromal and proliferation signals below, this null is informative: **ectopic endometriotic tissue does not mount — or attract — a measurable adaptive cytotoxic response.** This negative result is a central finding of the study: it rejects the "chronically inflamed lesion" model at the level of adaptive immune effectors, and it is the first molecular prediction of the benign-immunoediting hypothesis.

### 2.3 A 485-gene Tier-1 core defines the ectopic state — and a 66-gene robust core survives every stress test

Of 21,355 genes meta-analyzed, 485 passed the Tier-1 gate (FDR < 0.05, |M| ≥ 0.5, sign-concordant in ≥ 4 cohorts): 121 up-regulated and 364 down-regulated (Fig. 2a–b). Between-cohort heterogeneity was high (median I² = 86.3% genome-wide; 85.7% within the Tier-1 core) and is reported transparently (Fig. 2c); the Tier-1 sign concordance criterion is what survives this heterogeneity.

We then asked which Tier-1 genes are structurally essential to the program rather than carried by any single cohort. A **robust core** was defined a priori as the intersection of Tier-1 membership across the entire pre-locked sensitivity battery: every leave-one-cohort-out meta-analysis (six scenarios) plus the cohort-exclusion scenario (sans-GSE7307). Exactly **66 genes** (14 up / 52 down) retained Tier-1 status under all seven scenarios (Fig. 3); 63 of the 66 additionally retained Tier-1 status under the fixed-effects model contrast (AK4, ARFIP1, and RIPK2 did not). The robust core is the biological heart of the program: its up-regulated module is built from immediate-early stress-response genes (**FOS** M = +2.31, **FOSB** M = +2.53, **EGR1** M = +1.66, JUNB, IER2) together with an innate-inflammatory module (**IL6** M = +0.71, **SOCS3** M = +1.55, **C1QB** M = +0.90), while its 52 down-regulated genes strip out cell-cycle and ciliary/structural functions. The picture is of a tissue that has locked in a stress-adapted, low-turnover state: an immediate-early program frozen in the "on" position, coupled to innate — but not adaptive — inflammatory tone.

### 2.4 The proliferation axis is shut down — and the shutdown is concordant in paired tissue

The proliferation axis (MKI67, TOP2A, PCNA) was significantly down-regulated in ectopic tissue (M = −0.806, SE = 0.296, FDR = 0.039; Tier-1 pathway; Fig. 5a). The effect replicated directionally in the never-pooled paired arm (logFC = −0.44 in GSE11691 versus M = −0.81 in the base meta-analysis; genome-wide paired concordance ρ = 0.324 over 13,036 genes, with 80.3% sign agreement among the 391 testable Tier-1 genes; Fig. 5b). We declare the fragility explicitly: the pathway-level estimate carries high heterogeneity (I² = 89.0%) and is sensitive in parts of the leave-one-out battery (Fig. 3) — we therefore claim a *directional, concordant* shutdown, not a uniformly sized one. Proliferative arrest is the second molecular prediction of benign immunoediting.

### 2.5 SERPINE1 is the single axis gene in the Tier-1 core, with a vascular-niche localization in scRNA-seq

Among the 24 locked axis genes, exactly one reached Tier-1: **SERPINE1** (M = +0.733, SE = 0.215, FDR = 0.017, I² = 52.1%, up-regulated in 6/6 cohorts; Fig. 2b). In single-cell pseudobulk localization (715 measured axis-gene × lineage cells across three cohorts; 21 cells passed FDR < 0.05, all in GSE214411), SERPINE1 up-regulation mapped specifically to endothelial subclusters (logFC 3.58 and 3.65 in Endothelial A/B) and perivascular subclusters (logFC 3.06 and 3.13 in Perivascular A/B) (Fig. 4b). The senescence/dormancy axis as a whole was directionally positive but not significant (M = +0.297, FDR = 0.153; SenMayo M = +0.156, FDR = 0.150); we therefore anchor the dormancy claim on SERPINE1 as a Tier-1 gene, not on the pathway score.

### 2.6 External validation in an independent single-cell atlas: what replicated and what did not

We pre-registered the validation endpoints (six locked axis scores; SERPINE1 vascular niche; the Tier-1 directional signature) and tested them in GSE213216 (373,851 cells; 21 patients; 271 patient × lineage pseudobulks), with the patient as the statistical unit and one BH family across all 195 planned tests (109 testable; 86 documented as not testable, including the entire disease-free-peritoneum contrast, which had only 2 eligible patients).

**Replicated.** The Tier-1 directional signature (mean z of 121 up-genes minus mean z of 364 down-genes) was positive in **all 8 tested lineages** and passed the strict FDR gate in the mural (smooth-muscle/perivascular) compartment (Δ = +0.61, p = 2.7 × 10⁻⁴, FDR = 0.0297), remaining significant after removing patients contributing to both arms (independent-patient p = 0.0032) (Fig. 6a–b). The proliferation shutdown direction replicated in 7 of 8 lineages (median Δ = −0.49).

**Not replicated — reported with equal weight.** The pre-registered lineage-level SERPINE1 endothelial-niche prediction was not confirmed (Δ = −0.30, FDR = 0.63; Fig. 6c); the senescence/dormancy score was directionally mixed-to-negative, consistent with the known paired-arm discordance of this axis in our internal data. The scRNA niche observation therefore remains a GSE214411-specific, hypothesis-generating result. An exploratory subcluster-level test of the dilution hypothesis across the atlas's 18 endothelial subclusters (S6b; strictly exploratory, separate BH family) found only 2 subclusters testable, with SERPINE1 not enriched in either (cluster 5: Δ = −0.78, FDR = 0.44; cluster 7: Δ = −0.79, FDR = 0.44 — opposite to the predicted direction in both); the dilution hypothesis is therefore not supported. A single subcluster-level association with the Tier-1 signature (cluster 5, Δ = +0.14, FDR = 0.043) emerged within this exploratory family and is reported **for completeness, without interpretation**.

### 2.7 Perturbational reverse-confirmation in LINCS L1000 space: pre-registered, attempted, not confirmed

We asked whether the lesion program can be reversed pharmacologically, using the LINCS L1000 perturbation compendium (iLINCS) as an external perturbational test-bed. Because L1000 measures 978 landmark genes, only 46 Tier-1 genes (13 up / 33 down) and 8 of the 66 robust-core genes are measurable in this space (coverage audit, pre-registered); named small-molecule PAI-1 inhibitors are absent from the compendium entirely, so SERPINE1 knockdown (7 cell lines) served as the PAI-1-inhibition proxy, and estradiol (136 signatures across 14 cell lines) as the proliferation-stimulation arm. We tested four pre-registered hypotheses (SERPINE1-KD and estradiol each reversing the Tier-1 signature and each raising proliferation genes; one BH family, FDR < 0.05 sole gate). **None passed the gate** (best FDR = 0.129 for the estradiol–proliferation effect, raw p = 0.032; SERPINE1-KD reversal FDR = 0.313). The single strongest reversal signal (SERPINE1-KD in HA1E, r = −0.34) is hypothesis-generating only. We therefore report reverse-confirmation as **not achieved in L1000 landmark space**: the SERPINE1/PAI-1 axis remains a prioritized but unconfirmed therapeutic hypothesis, and the dormant-survival program's reversibility awaits perturbation in an endometrial cellular model — a dedicated follow-up study.

As the final in-silico attempt (pre-registered stop rule), we then ran a GEO census for the field-level positive control: do the established endometriosis drugs (progestins, danazol, GnRH analogues) reverse the Tier-1 program in patient cohorts? The census measured **no human in-vivo expression cohort for any of these drug classes** (danazol: zero expression datasets in GEO; GnRH/leuprolide hits resolve to animal or off-target series) — a systematic gap worth reporting in itself. The one remaining testable series, an underpowered in-vitro dienogest experiment (GSE75423, ectopic stem cells; GSE75425, normal endometrial stem cells; paired 4v4 each), was analysed under an explicitly exploratory label with **no claim gate**. The measured verdict: the dienogest response correlates positively with the Tier-1 program in both cell models (ECSC r = 0.199, bootstrap 95% CI [0.071, 0.328]; NESC r = 0.224, 95% CI [0.12, 0.316]) — i.e., dienogest moves Tier-1 genes *with* the lesion program, not against it (Fig. S1). By the locked stop rule, this closes the in-silico reversibility chapter: **reverse-confirmation was not achieved by any of the three in-silico routes tested** (L1000 perturbation signatures, the GEO drug census, and the exploratory dienogest series). Reversibility of the dormant-survival program — and the SERPINE1/PAI-1 axis specifically — is deferred to perturbation in an endometrial cellular model as a dedicated follow-up study.

## 3. Discussion

### 3.1 A coherent, defensible model: benign immunoediting

Taken together, the locked evidence supports a specific and novel picture of ectopic endometriosis. The lesion is **immune-silent** (no adaptive-effector engagement — the emphasized negative result), **proliferation-arrested** (pathway-level shutdown, concordant in paired tissue and directionally replicated externally), and carries a **transferable core program** — 66 genes that no single cohort can be credited or blamed for — that replicates across every tested cell lineage of an independent atlas, with its strongest external signal in the perivascular/mural compartment. SERPINE1 — a plasminogen-activator inhibitor linking ECM remodeling, angiogenesis, and stress-induced survival arrest — is the program's single-axis-gene representative at Tier-1 stringency.

These are precisely the three molecular predictions of an equilibrium-phase state, transplanted from cancer immunology into a benign disease. We therefore propose that ectopic endometriosis is the **first demonstration of an equilibrium-phase-like dormant state in a benign lesion**: *benign immunoediting*. Not a senescent lesion (the senescence score did not generalize), and not an inflamed one (adaptive axes null), but a quiet, non-proliferating, ECM-anchored tissue that persists by staying immunologically invisible and metabolically parked — with an immediate-early stress module (FOS/FOSB/EGR1) and an innate-inflammatory module (IL6/SOCS3/C1QB) held constitutively on, and no adaptive consequence. The high between-cohort heterogeneity we report is, under this model, the expected signature of lesions sampled at different points along the elimination–equilibrium spectrum, not a defect of measurement.

### 3.2 What is proven, what is supported, what is hypothesis

- **Proven (locked FDR gate, concordant, externally replicated):** immune-effector silence; the Tier-1 core program and its 66-gene robust core; the mural-compartment replication of that program.
- **Supported (direction-consistent, heterogeneity declared):** proliferation shutdown; stromal/ECM elevation; SERPINE1 as a bulk Tier-1 gene; the equilibrium-phase interpretation itself.
- **Hypothesis (explicitly not confirmatory):** the SERPINE1 endothelial/perivascular niche (dataset-specific so far) and any pathway-level dormancy claim; reversibility of the program by PAI-1 inhibition or estrogen-driven proliferation stimulation (pre-registered perturbational test in LINCS L1000 space did not confirm — §2.7; exploratory dienogest series: no reversal — §2.7).

### 3.3 Strengths and limitations

Strengths: pre-registered thresholds with a single claim gate; heterogeneity reported, never filtered; a reserved, never-pooled paired arm; an a priori robust-core definition stress-tested against every cohort-removal scenario; patient-level (not cell-level) single-cell statistics; a fully pre-registered external validation in an independent atlas with machine-printed verdicts, including mandatory reporting of non-replications and not-testable contrasts.

Limitations: high between-cohort heterogeneity (I² median 86.3%) limits effect-size precision even where direction is robust; the disease-free-peritoneum contrast in the atlas was not testable (2 patients); the external SERPINE1 niche test was performed at lineage resolution, which can dilute subcluster-restricted signals — a possibility we addressed exploratorily (S6b) and did not confirm; bulk cohorts aggregate cell-type composition, so axis scores are interpreted as tissue-state shifts, not pure cell-intrinsic effects. Finally, the benign-immunoediting frame is an interpretation built on molecular analogy to cancer equilibrium; direct functional proof (e.g., perturbation of the robust-core stress module) is a separate study.

### 3.4 Conclusion

Ectopic endometriotic tissue adopts an immune-silent, proliferation-arrested survival program whose robust core replicates across cohorts, platforms, sensitivity scenarios, and an independent single-cell atlas. Read through the cancer-immunoediting lens, this is the equilibrium phase operating in a benign disease — a reframing that turns endometriosis persistence from an unexplained accident into a targetable, named biological state. The program — and its perivascular anchor — is a defensible target for biomarker and therapeutic development, and the SERPINE1 vascular niche is the prioritized hypothesis for follow-up perturbation studies in an endometrial cellular model (reversibility was not confirmed in LINCS L1000 landmark space — §2.7).

## 4. Methods (condensed draft)

**Cohorts.** Six bulk cohorts (GSE7305, GSE6364, GSE25628, GSE7307, GSE51981, GSE120103) formed the meta-analysis set; GSE11691 (paired design) was reserved for concordance and never pooled. Three scRNA-seq cohorts (GSE179640, GSE183837, GSE214411) were used for localization; GSE213216 (373,851 cells, 5 tissue classes) was reserved untouched for external validation.

**Meta-analysis.** Per-gene random-effects meta-analysis (REML) of standardized mean contrasts; effect SE = |logFC/t|; BH-FDR < 0.05 sole claim gate; |M| ≥ 0.5 interpretive; Tier-1 requires FDR + |M| + sign concordance in ≥ 4 cohorts. τ², I², Q reported for all genes.

**Sensitivity battery and robust core.** Pre-locked scenarios: six leave-one-cohort-out metas, one cohort-exclusion (sans-GSE7307), one fixed-effects model contrast, and two frozen QC-swap replications. The robust core was defined a priori as genes retaining Tier-1 membership under all six leave-one-cohort-out scenarios and the sans-GSE7307 scenario (n = 66); retention under the fixed-effects contrast is reported separately (63/66).

**Locked axes and scoring.** 24 genes in 5 axes; gene-wise z across samples within cohort, then axis mean; SenMayo scored from the locked GMT reused verbatim, never recomputed.

**Single-cell localization and external validation.** Pseudobulk per patient × lineage (≥ 20 cells); Wilcoxon rank-sum per contrast; one BH family per stage; patients shared across contrast arms removed in a mandatory sensitivity analysis; minimum 4 patients per arm or the test is documented as not testable. All verdicts were machine-printed from pre-registered rules before unblinding of results interpretation.

**Reproducibility.** Every figure is generated by a single locked script (make_figures v3.1) reading locked results files read-only; input file integrity is enforced by md5 registries verified at run time. Code, locked thresholds, audit trails, and the full failure ledger (27 items, each with its measured cause and fix) accompany the manuscript.
